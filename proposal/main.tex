\section*{Background and Motivation}


Almost all modern aerial vehicles rely on GPS for navigation. Failures in GPS due to signal interference, jamming, or other technical issues can compromise safety. In such cases, alternative navigation methods that use visual geospatial features become critical. Current solutions often rely on specific scene recognition, which may falter in dynamic environments. Having many distinctive features in many scenes is much better than only one or two unique objects for reference.

This research draws inspiration from urban morphology and street network analysis. Louf and Barthelemy's classification of street patterns\cite{remi_2014} offers a macro-level understanding of urban layouts, while Boeing's entropy metrics \cite{Boeing_2019} provide a framework for quantifying spatial order and complexity. These approaches help identify geospatially distinctive regions, such as settlements, infrastructure, and shaped parks with numerous unique objects, that could serve as potential reference points in GPS-denied scenarios.

By leveraging OpenStreetMap (OSM) data and geospatial analysis techniques\cite{Boeing2017}, this research aims to determine the feasibility of identifying such scenes and to analyze their distribution across selected areas. This study bridges the gap between theoretical urban studies and practical geospatial needs in UAV.

\section*{Problem Formulation}


UAVs lack a robust framework for pre-identifying geospatially distinctive scenes that could serve as reference points. This research focuses on the feasibility of identifying such characterized by high visual distinctiveness scenes and analyzing their distribution across a country, administrative area, or specific region. These insights could be used in future projects aimed at creating fail-safe navigation routes for UAVs.

\section*{Project Aims and Deliverables}
\subsection*{Aims}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Identifying geospatially distinctive scenes using publicly available geospatial datasets.
    \item Quantifying the distribution of such scenes across a country, administrative area, or custom region.
    \item Classifying these scenes based on their recognizability in an orthophoto perspective.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Deliverables}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item  A systematic methodology to classify scenes based on their recognizability.
    \item A quantified dataset of selected areas.
    \item Statistical analysis and visualizations of the distribution of these scenes within the areas.
    \item A final thesis document detailing the methodology, results, conclusions, and feasibility analysis.
\end{enumerate}

\section*{Project Timeline}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Month 1:} Conduct a literature review and extract OSM data for selected areas. Explore metrics such as intersection density, street orientation, and entropy.
    \item \textbf{Month 2:} Develop methods to identify geospatially distinctive regions. Perform initial analysis on selected study areas.
    \item \textbf{Month 3:} Conduct a detailed analysis of region distribution. Visualize the results using GIS tools.
    \item \textbf{Month 4:} Refine the methodology and validate the results. Begin writing the thesis document.
    \item \textbf{Month 5:} Complete the thesis document and prepare the defense presentation, including visuals.
\end{itemize}
